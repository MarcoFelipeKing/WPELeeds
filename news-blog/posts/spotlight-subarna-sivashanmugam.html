Decarbonising Concrete and Cement: Life Cycle Assessment & Circular Construction

Dr Subarna Sivashanmugam Research Assistant in Low-Carbon Construction and Circular Economy • BioResources & the Circular Economy

Concrete is the most widely used construction material on Earth, and Portland cement production accounts for around 7 to 8% of global carbon dioxide emissions. Dr Subarna Sivashanmugam, Research Assistant in the School of Civil Engineering at the University of Leeds, combines Building Information Modelling (BIM), construction waste minimisation, and environmental life cycle assessment (LCA) to evaluate how structural design choices and circular alternative cements can cut the embodied carbon of the built environment.

1. Cast-in-situ versus composite reinforced concrete slabs (EPSRC IAA)

Comparing the whole-life environmental and cost impacts of cast-in-situ and composite reinforced concrete floor slab systems.

Supported by the EPSRC Impact Acceleration Account (IAA) on the project Environmental and Cost Impacts of Using Low Carbon Concrete for Slabs Construction (led by Dr K. Tsavdaridis, Prof. Theodore Hanein, and Dr Anne Velenturf), Dr Sivashanmugam evaluates the environmental and financial trade-offs between traditional cast-in-situ reinforced concrete slabs and composite slab systems.

Because floor slabs represent a major share of the structural mass in multi-storey buildings, integrating BIM-based material quantities with cradle-to-grave life cycle assessment allows engineers and contractors to identify where low-carbon concrete mixes and composite structural forms achieve genuine reductions in whole-life carbon without shifting burdens elsewhere in the construction cycle.

2. Belite-calcium sulfoaluminate (BCSA) cement from industrial by-products (UKRI FLF)

Evaluating the embodied carbon and circularity of belite-calcium sulfoaluminate (BCSA) cements produced using industrial mineral wastes.

Alongside structural slab optimisation, Dr Sivashanmugam contributes to the UKRI Future Leaders Fellowship (FLF) programme led by Prof. Theodore Hanein on Green Cement Production from Industrial By-Products: Energy-Efficient and Low-Carbon Belite-Calcium Sulfoaluminate (BCSA).

BCSA cements require lower kilning temperatures and release less limestone-derived CO₂ than ordinary Portland cement, and can be synthesised using alumina- and sulfate-bearing industrial wastes and by-products. Dr Sivashanmugam's life cycle assessment work quantifies the cradle-to-gate embodied carbon and resource circularity of BCSA formulations across raw material sourcing, kiln energy demand, and supply-chain transport.

Connecting BIM, construction waste, and the circular economy

Building on her doctoral research in Building Information Modelling (BIM) and construction waste management, Dr Sivashanmugam's work sits within WPE's BioResources & the Circular Economy theme and our cross-cutting Data-Driven & Computational Methods capability, providing quantitative life-cycle evidence to guide circular construction standards and industrial decarbonisation.
