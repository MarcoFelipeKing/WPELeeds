The Water, Public Health and Environmental Engineering (WPE) group organises its research around four interconnected themes — Indoor Air & Healthy Buildings, BioResources & Circular Economy, Sanitation & WASH, and Water — supported by a cross-cutting capability in Experimental, Microbiological, and Computational Methods.

Our interdisciplinary approach combines building physics, fluid mechanics, environmental microbiology, analytical chemistry, public health engineering, and social science to deliver practical solutions with global impact.

Indoor Air Quality and Airborne Infection

Engineering Healthy, Low-Carbon Buildings

With people spending up to 90% of their time indoors, our Indoor Air research theme investigates how respiratory pathogens and air pollutants move through buildings, how ventilation and air-cleaning technologies reduce exposure, and how net-zero building retrofits can protect occupant health.

Anchored by our walk-in controlled aerobiology chamber and high-performance CFD modelling, our work has directly shaped UK SAGE, NHS, CIBSE, and World Health Organization ventilation guidance.

Research Areas

Airborne Infection & Bioaerosols

Chamber experiments, single-particle microfluidic bioaerosol detection, and QMRA modelling across airborne and surface-contact routes.

Ventilation & Air Cleaning

Optimising hospital and school ventilation, HEPA filtration, and germicidal UV-C (254 nm and 222 nm far-UVC) systems.

Building Physics & Net Zero

Balancing indoor air quality and thermal comfort with low-carbon heating, heat pumps, and building energy efficiency.

Sensing & Environmental Justice

Deploying low-cost indoor air quality sensor networks in schools and homes to address exposure inequalities.

Group Director & Theme Contact

Associate Professor Marco-Felipe King

Director of WPE; Associate Professor in Building Engineering and Infection Transmission; Co-Director, EPSRC CDT in Future Fluid Dynamics

Associate Professor King investigates how ventilation strategies, building design, and occupant behaviour govern indoor air quality and pathogen transmission. His work combines computational fluid dynamics (CFD) modelling, Bayesian inference, and experimental studies in the Leeds aerobiology chamber to reduce healthcare-associated and community infections in partnership with the NHS, government departments, and industry.

BioResources and Circular Economy

Transforming Waste into Clean Energy, Nutrients, and Materials

The BioResources and Circular Economy theme develops biological, chemical, and systems-level solutions to recover biomethane, biohydrogen, nutrients, protein, and secondary construction materials from organic and industrial waste streams.

From the £1.75M Ayrton Challenge BioCool off-grid milk-cooling programme in Uganda to circular economy policy for offshore wind infrastructure and the UK foundation industries, we link bioprocess engineering with circular governance.

Explore BioResources BioCool Project Highlight

Research Areas

Waste Valorisation & Biogas

Anaerobic digestion, biomethane upgrading, biohydrogen, and hydrothermal carbonisation of agricultural and municipal wastes.

Microalgae & Duckweed Biorefineries

Harnessing photobioreactors, microalgae, and duckweed to capture carbon, remove micropollutants, and recover protein.

Nutrient Recovery & Digestate

Extracting nitrogen, phosphorus, and safe biofertilisers from wastewater, faecal sludge, and anaerobic digestate.

Circular Economy & Infrastructure

Whole-system resource flows, offshore wind decommissioning, and circular transitions in the foundation industries.

Sanitation and WASH

Safely Managed, Climate-Resilient Sanitation and Hygiene

The Sanitation and WASH theme works across the entire sanitation service chain — from containment and emptying to treatment, governance, and public finance — in rapidly growing cities and rural communities across Africa, South and Southeast Asia, and the UK.

Home to the Shit Flow Diagram (SFD) and leads on the FCDO WASH Systems for Health and DFAT EMISI programmes, our team combines public health engineering with political economy and climate-resilience modelling.

Water

Water Supply, Treatment, Hydraulics, and Flood Resilience

The Water theme works across the full water cycle — from global flood risk, river hydrodynamics, and pressurised pipeline transients to drinking-water microplastics removal, constructed wetlands, and subsurface contaminant geochemistry.

Closely integrated with water@leeds (directed by Prof. Martin Tillotson, with Prof. Mark Trigg as Associate Director), we partner with utilities, regulators, and international agencies to build resilient water systems.

Experimental, Microbiological & Computational Methods

One Shared Toolkit Across All Four Themes

Physics tells us how the world should behave; experimental and field data tell us how it actually behaved. Across indoor air, water, sanitation, and bioresources, our group shares specialist capabilities in walk-in chamber experimentation, Class II environmental microbiology, automated analytical chemistry, high-performance CFD, physics-informed machine learning, Bayesian calibration, and Quantitative Microbial Risk Assessment (QMRA).
